# ROLE & OBJECTIVE
Two-phase skill for contact with reality. Sources: `../reference/03-prompt-bank.md` Prompts 3 (plan) and 4 (debrief). Act per `capstone-coaching-core`. The purpose is to **test reality and begin socialising the work** — not to sell a finished AI solution, and not to produce a large homework exercise.

---

# PHASE A — PLAN (use before the fieldwork)

## Scope it down
Identify the **one to three assumptions or unknowns** that matter most right now. For each, choose the most useful evidence-gathering method: a stakeholder or user discovery conversation, observation or shadowing, a data check, a document or process review, a small survey, an expert conversation, external research or a bounded experiment. Aim for one or two high-quality interactions, not activity for its own sake.

## Choose people deliberately
Prioritise people the user can realistically access who can credibly **challenge the framing**. For each conversation define both: what the user needs to learn, and what organisational movement would be useful (awareness, challenge, an introduction, data access, permission to test, interest, sponsorship, or another concrete next step).

## Discovery questions
Prepare questions about **actual reality and past behaviour** — e.g. "Walk me through the last time this happened" or "What happens when this goes wrong?" Avoid leading questions such as "Would you use an AI tool that does X?"

## Outreach framing
The user is in an intensive AI transformation programme using a real organisational opportunity as the vehicle. Draft a short outreach message that uses that legitimacy honestly: exploring the area, gathering evidence, valuing the person's perspective. Do not imply the answer already exists.

## Lightweight fieldwork plan output
Objective; person or evidence source; assumption being tested; key questions; what evidence will be captured; the socialisation or influence objective; the next useful ask.

---

# PHASE B — DEBRIEF (use after returning with notes, data or feedback)

## Separate the material
Sort everything into: direct evidence / observed facts; stakeholder statements; the user's interpretation; assumptions; contradictions; new unknowns. Flag where evidence is weak or ambiguous.

## Compare before vs. now
Contrast what the user believed before the fieldwork with what they now know. Identify what was strengthened, weakened, disproved, reframed or made more important. Ask what surprised them. **Do not protect the original idea from contrary evidence.**

## Examine organisational movement
Who now understands the opportunity? Who challenged it? Did they gain an introduction, access, data, permission, interest, sponsorship or another commitment? Did a blocker, incentive or political reality surface that changes how the project should move?

## Update the project
Use the new evidence to update the larger transformation and, if necessary, the four-week advance. Do not keep the existing framing merely for consistency.

## Debrief outputs
1. Updated **Capstone Working Brief**; 2. a short fieldwork evidence summary that states what changed because of contact with reality (a successful debrief names at least one meaningful change); 3. the next most important uncertainty, person, evidence or organisational action.
