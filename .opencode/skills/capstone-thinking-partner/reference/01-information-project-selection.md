**Information About Expertise: Selecting Transformation Opportunities**

Raw material to inform a codified guide

This document is intentionally not yet the final codified expertise. It
captures the information base: principles, observations and practitioner
insights that inform how to choose a worthwhile transformation
opportunity. It should be refined into a codified guide before being
used as AI coaching context.

**1. The professional judgement we are trying to support**

The judgement is: which organisational transformation opportunity is
worth pursuing, and what should be learned, demonstrated or established
first to move it forward responsibly?

**2. Recurring insights from transformation practice**

  **Insight**                  **Why it matters**
  ---------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Value before technology      Start with the organisational objective and the value to be created, not the AI tool.
  Challenge before optimise    Do not assume the current process is the right thing to automate. Ask whether work should be improved, redesigned or reinvented.
  Whole-system thinking        AI transformation can change decisions, roles, teams, services, data, controls and operating models, not only workflows.
  Evidence over slideware      Ideas should move towards observable evidence, experiments, tests, implementation decisions or stakeholder learning.
  Responsibility by design     Risk, data, permissions, human oversight and stakeholder consequences matter from the beginning.
  Cross-disciplinary reality   Business, technology, data, risk, security, legal, operations and users may all affect whether an opportunity can work.

**3. Practitioner heuristics worth preserving**

> • An opportunity does not have to be flashy to be valuable.
> Repetitive, mundane work can be strategically important if it consumes
> scarce capacity or prevents higher-value work.
>
> • A project copied from another organisation may be inappropriate if
> the context, incentives, data, processes or constraints differ.
>
> • A smaller initiative within a real sphere of influence can be
> preferable to a large initiative that cannot move.
>
> • Showing an outcome often changes the conversation more than
> explaining an idea. What is shown need not be an app; it can be
> evidence, a redesigned process, a quantified baseline, a tested
> workflow or a concrete decision.
>
> • The gap between experimentation and production should be
> acknowledged early, but should not prevent learning through bounded
> action.
>
> • The first useful advance may be validation, quantified evidence,
> sponsorship, data access or decision clarity rather than technology.

**4. Common weak forms of reasoning**

  **Weak reasoning**                               **Why it is insufficient**
  ------------------------------------------------ -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  We should use AI for this.                       This starts with the tool rather than the outcome.
  Another organisation is doing it.                Context may be different. Copying is not evidence of fit.
  It will save time.                               Time saved is vague unless the baseline, beneficiaries and value of released capacity are understood.
  We can build a prototype quickly.                Technical feasibility is not the same as organisational value or adoption.
  This process exists, so we should automate it.   The process may itself be the thing that needs to change.
  People will obviously use it.                    Adoption depends on incentives, trust, workflow fit and perceived consequences.

**5. Important trade-offs**

  **Trade-off**                          **Professional implication**
  -------------------------------------- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Importance vs influence                A huge opportunity may be poor for a capstone if the participant cannot access evidence or move it forward.
  Ambition vs tractability               A high-ambition transformation needs a credible next advance, not necessarily a full implementation.
  Demonstrability vs depth               A visible demo can help, but shallow technology can distract from deeper organisational issues.
  Speed vs confidence                    Fast progress is useful only if it reduces the right uncertainty.
  Local improvement vs system redesign   Optimising one task may shift bottlenecks elsewhere.

**6. What this information still lacks**

This material is useful, but not yet enough. To become operational, it
must be turned into codified expertise: decision rules, diagnostic
questions, warning signs, context dependencies and examples that an AI
coach can apply consistently.
