- factor model
  - rn=∑kXnkfk+unr_n = \sum_{k} X_{nk}f_k +u_nrn=∑kXnkfk+un
  - the factors capture all systematic drivers of asset returns
    - specific returns are mutually uncorrelated
  - problems of modelling
    - omit important factors
    - indiscriminately add weak or spurious factors
      - make the model more susceptible to noise
      - statistical significance of the factor returns
    - Stability
      - factor stability coefficient
        - cross-sectional correlation of factor exposures from one month to the next
    - Collinearity
      - Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)
        - regress a single factor against the other factors in the model:
        - VIF is defined through the explanatory power of the regression, as measured by R-squared
      - solution
        - orthogonalization

  - industry factor
    - basic criteria of industry factor selection
      - groupings of industries into factors must be economically intuitive
      - industry factors should have a strong degree of statistical significance
      - incorporating an additional industry factor should significantly increase the explanatory power of the model
      - thin industries (those with few assets) should be avoided
    - multiple-Industry Exposures
      - use Assets/Sales as the explanatory variable

  - factor covariance matrix
    - estimation
      - step 1
        - compute the factor correlation matrix from the set of daily factor returns
        - exponentially weighted averages
          - correlation half-life parameter
        - selecting the correlation half-life
          - sufficiently long
            - he effective number of observations T is significantly greater than the number of factors K
          - not too long
            - undue weight is placed on distant observations that have little relation to current market conditions
        - serial correlation
          - Newey-West methodology
        - case of missing factor returns
          - EM algorithm
      - step 2
        - compute factor volatilities
        - exponentially weighted averages, with half-life parameter
    - Eigenfactor Risk Adjustment
      - systematic tendency to underpredict the risk
      - cause of the bias
        - estimation error
      - σtrue=σpred1−K/T \sigma_{true} = \frac{\sigma_{pred}}{1-K/T} σtrue=1−K/Tσpred
    - Volatility Regime Adjustment
      - use cross-sectional observations to produce timelier and more accurate forecasts of factor volatility
        - basic intuition
          - the returns and risk forecasts of the other factors provide additional information for refining the volatility estimates.
      - standardized return : fkt/σktf_{kt} /\sigma_{kt}fkt/σkt
        - should have standard deviation close to 1 if the risk forecasts are accurate
      - cross-sectional standard deviation
        - factor cross-sectional bias statistic
          - BtF=1K∑k(fktσkt)2B_t^F = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k} \left(\frac{f_{kt}}{\sigma_{kt}} \right)^2 }BtF=K1∑k(σktfkt)2
          - K is the total number of factors
        - factor volatility multiplier
          - λF=∑t(BtF)2wi\lambda_F = \sqrt{ \sum_{t} (B_t^F)^2w_i } λF=∑t(BtF)2wi
          - wiw_iwi
            - an exponential weight with VRA half-life
            - primary determinant of model responsiveness for factor risk
        - VRA forecasts
          - σ~k=λFσk\tilde{\sigma}_k = \lambda_F \sigma_k σ~k=λFσk
