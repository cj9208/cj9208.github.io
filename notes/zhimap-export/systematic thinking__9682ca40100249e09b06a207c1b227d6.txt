- example
  - Data-driven Hedging of Stock Index Options via Deep Learning
  - cursory reading
    - title
      - topic: option hedging
      - object: stock index option
      - methof: data-driven
      - tools: deep learning
    - abstract
      - features
        - time to maturity, the Black-Scholes delta and market sentiment
        - different combinations
      - daily hedging
      - results
        - signiﬁcantly outperforms delta hedging and a data-driven hedging model
        - demonstrate the importance of market sentiment for hedging
    - introducation
      - details of two previous methods
      - development and achievement of deep learnning
    - tables and figures
      - details of feature combinations
      - results of different models
    - conclusion
      - as in abstracts
  - comprehensive reading
    - questions
      - what about individual options?
      - why daily hedging?
      - deep learning
        - what is the structure?
      - features
        - why thest? any other?
          - option characteristics + market sentiment
        - what is sentiment factors?
          - VIX for call, return for puts
          - majority of calls and puts being traded are near the money or out of money (see Table 1). Changes in the daily prices of these calls are very sensitive to the volatility level, while changes in the daily prices of these puts are very sensitive to the index return.
        - second round
          - VIX represents market volatility, what about implied volatility?
          - is the authors' explanation for choice of sentiment factors plausive? in general, what do investors use options for?
      - experiment
        - settings
          - data source
          - details of model training
        - what measures did the author use for hedge performance
        - can the experiments support the results
        - the results align with previous papers?
      - limitations
